## 02_5.103_The_Naive_Bayes_Classifier_Video_4_min

# 5.103 The Naive Bayes Classifier
Video • 4 min - Summary

URL: https://www.coursera.org/learn/uol-cm3015-machine-learning-and-neural-networks/lecture/tMmoh/5-103-the-naive-bayes-classifier

This course provides a comprehensive progression through machine learning, beginning with fundamental concepts in Week 1 and 2. The curriculum first delves into supervised learning, dedicating Weeks 3 and 4 to the core principles of Classification. This is followed by a focused study on Regression in Weeks 5 and 6.

Weeks 7 and 8 transition to advanced concepts, covering Model Evaluation and Improvement, culminating in Probabilistic Classification. A significant focus is placed on Bayesian classification (5.101), emphasizing Bayes' theorem (5.102) and the practical application of the Naive Bayes Classifier (5.103, 5.104).

The learning structure includes specialized labs, such as the Naive Bayes Lab (5.201), and extensive further reading (5.202). Moving into unsupervised learning in Weeks 9 and 10, the focus shifts to pattern recognition without labeled data.

The course heavily emphasizes deep learning, initiating discussions on Neural Networks and Deep Learning in Week 11. Subsequent weeks (12 through 14) are dedicated to mastering the mathematical building blocks of these complex networks. Weeks 15 through 18 reinforce this knowledge through practical application of getting started with neural networks.

The final weeks (19 and 20) serve as a robust review, revisiting the overall Fundamentals of Machine Learning to consolidate all learned concepts.

The content of this specific topic is described as being largely self-contained. For a supplementary resource, the course textbook authored by Chollet includes a small dedicated section on Naive Bayes, found under section 1.2.1. When seeking further reading on Probabilistic modelling, students are directed to the work of Ethem Alpaydin. Specifically, Alpaydin's book, *Introduction to machine learning*, covers this subject in sections 3.1 and 3.2. The publication details for this text are: Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2014, and it is the 3rd edition, bearing ISBN 9780262028189. An extensive resource for deep study on probabilistic modelling is offered by K. Murphy. Murphy's volume is titled *Machine learning: a probabilistic perspective*. The publication details for Murphy's book are: Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2012, and its ISBN is 9780262018029. For a strong introductory perspective on the current topic, the beginning of Chapter 2 of Alpaydin's book is recommended. This initial material covers sections 2.1 to 2.2, providing a good introduction to probabilistic modelling concepts. Students are encouraged to practice by attempting the exercises numbered 2.1 through 2.9. Generative modelling and Bayes are covered in the beginning of Chapter 3 of Alpaydin’s work. This coverage specifically includes sections 3.1 through 3.2.4. To read about Naïve Bayes specifically, one can skip to section 3.5 of Alpaydin’s book.
